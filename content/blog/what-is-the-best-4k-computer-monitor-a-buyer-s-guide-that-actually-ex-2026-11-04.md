## Why the monitor-versus-TV boundary is blurring

Walk into the question naively and you'll find that "computer monitor" and "4K TV" have converged to the point where category labels matter less than panel physics. ## Brightness is the spec that separates good from great

Here's the number most spec sheets bury: genuine HDR requires at least 1,000 nits of peak brightness. Below that threshold, an "HDR" label is marketing, not a capability — the display physically cannot produce the dynamic range HDR content encodes.

That's why the Hisense U8N's 3,000 nits peak brightness (per BuyWhere's catalog data) puts it in a different class from entry-level QLED panels like the TCL Q6, which tops out near 600 nits. At a desk, brightness matters differently than in a living room: a 600-nit panel is fine in a dim office but washes out in a sunlit room, and it can't render HDR highlights — the glint on a window in your 4K footage, the sun in a game — at anything close to true intensity. Mini-LED backlighting, which is what the U7N and U8N use, subdivides the backlight into hundreds of local dimming zones so a bright specular highlight doesn't bloom across the whole screen. For photo and video work where you judge exposure on-screen, that difference is not subtle.

## Input ports are where buyers get burned

Count your devices before you buy, then count the ports twice. The port hierarchy in the current 4K QLED lineup, per BuyWhere's product data, looks like this:

- **Samsung QN85D and QN90D**: 4 HDMI 2.1 ports each
- **Hisense U7N and U8N**: 2 HDMI 2.1 ports
- **TCL Q6 and Fire TV Omni QLED**: 1 HDMI 2.1 port

A single HDMI 2.1 port is a genuine problem if you run a desktop plus a console: HDMI 2.1 is what carries 4K at 120Hz with variable refresh rate, and you don't want to swap cables every session. The older HDMI 2.0 ports on the same panels handle 4K60 fine for secondary devices, but your primary high-refresh source needs the 2.1 input. This is the single most common regret we see in returns: someone buys on panel quality, then discovers their setup needs a $150 HDMI switcher they hadn't budgeted for.

## Color: quantum dot everywhere, platforms nowhere

One spec you don't need to agonize over: all seven models BuyWhere tracks in this category use Quantum Dot film for wider color gamut. The QD layer converts LED backlight output into narrower, more saturated primaries, so color accuracy differences between these models come from calibration and processing, not from a fundamental panel-technology gap. If a listing claims its Quantum Dot panel is categorically more colorful than another's, be skeptical — the mechanism is identical.

The bigger day-to-day difference is the smart platform, which affects monitor users more than TV buyers expect. Samsung runs Tizen; Hisense and TCL run Google TV; the Fire TV Omni QLED runs Fire TV. If you use the display as a hybrid monitor/entertainment screen, the platform determines whether your streaming apps, casting, and voice search work the way you expect — and whether the interface fights you every time you switch inputs from your PC.
