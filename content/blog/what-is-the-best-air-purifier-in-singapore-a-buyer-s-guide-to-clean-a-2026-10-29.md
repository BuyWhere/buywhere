## Start with the room, not the brand

The single biggest mistake Singapore buyers make is shopping by brand first and room size last. An undersized purifier in a living room runs at maximum fan speed forever — loud, power-hungry, and still ineffective. An oversized one in a bedroom is wasted money and floor space in a typical HDB flat.

The mechanism that matters is clean air delivery rate (CADR), which measures how much filtered air a unit moves per hour. Manufacturers publish CADR figures and recommended room sizes, but the honest method is to measure your room, match it against the CADR-to-room-size guidance on the spec sheet, and buy with about 20% headroom — Singapore's haze episodes and perennial dust mean you want capacity to spare, not a unit running at its ceiling. A bedroom needs far less than an open-concept living-dining area, and the price gap between the right-sized unit and the flagship model is often large enough to fund two years of replacement filters.

This is exactly where a structured catalog matters. When you can pull spec-level data side by side instead of reading marketing copy listing by listing, the sizing decision becomes arithmetic rather than guesswork.

## Haze, humidity, and why Singapore is a special case

Singapore's air quality challenges are seasonal and structural at the same time. Transboundary haze from regional biomass burning can push PM2.5 levels up sharply during dry-season months, and the NEA's 24-hour PSI readings become part of daily life when it hits. A purifier bought casually in February gets stress-tested in September.

What that means practically: prioritize true HEPA (or equivalent) particulate filtration over gimmicks, and be skeptical of models that lean heavily on ionizers or UV as headline features — those are secondary mechanisms, while the filter media does the real work. Activated carbon layers matter too, because haze carries odor and gaseous pollutants that particulate filters alone don't capture.

Humidity is the second local factor. Filters held in consistently moist air degrade differently than in temperate climates, and replacement intervals published for, say, a Japanese or US market may not map cleanly onto a Singapore condo. Budget for more frequent filter changes than the box suggests, and factor that recurring cost into the 'best' decision. A cheaper unit with expensive, hard-to-source filters is a worse long-term buy than a mid-priced unit with widely stocked consumables — which brings us to availability.

## How to actually run the comparison

A disciplined purchase takes about twenty minutes:

1. **Measure your room** and note whether it's enclosed or open-plan — this sets your CADR target.
2. **Shortlist three to four models** in the right size class, filtering out ones whose filter SKUs don't appear on Shopee or Lazada from credible sellers.
3. **Compare live prices** for each shortlisted model across marketplaces and authorized retailers. Check both the unit price and the bundle (some listings include a spare filter, which changes the real cost math over two years).
4. **Time the purchase if you can.** If haze season is months away and no promotion is running, the sale-event discount is usually worth waiting for — the 20-35% swings seen in tracked appliance categories are not unusual.
