## The Budget Tier: TCL's Aggressive Play at $398

At the entry level of the QLED market, TCL has made the most aggressive move with its **55-inch Q6 QLED** priced at just $398 on Amazon. This undercuts the Hisense 55-inch U7N Series Mini-LED QLED 4K Smart TV, which sits at $498 at Walmart — a $100 gap that matters for budget-conscious shoppers. Both sets use Quantum Dot film for wide color gamuts, but the similarity ends there.

The TCL Q6 tops out at approximately 600 nits peak brightness, which falls well below what videophiles consider genuine HDR territory (that requires at least 1,000 nits). It also ships with just one HDMI 2.1 port, limiting your ability to connect a modern gaming console and a next-gen streaming device simultaneously. For casual viewers who want quantum dot color without the premium price, the TCL Q6 delivers — but you're making real trade-offs on brightness and connectivity.

## Mid-Range Showdown: TCL QM8 vs Samsung QN85D

Move up to the 65-inch segment and the competitive dynamics shift. The **TCL 65-inch QM8 QLED 4K Mini-LED Smart TV** comes in at $999 on Amazon, while the **Samsung 65-inch QN85D QLED 4K Smart TV** hits $1,197 at Best Buy — a $198 difference that makes TCL the clear value play in this bracket.

Both sets use Mini-LED backlighting and Quantum Dot technology, but the Samsung pulls ahead on connectivity: it offers four HDMI 2.1 ports versus the TCL's single HDMI 2.1 port on the Q6 (the QM8 specification is similar). For users with multiple devices — a PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X, and a soundbar — that port count difference is significant. Samsung also runs Tizen OS versus TCL's Google TV, which matters if you're already invested in one ecosystem.

The real question is whether that $198 gap justifies Samsung's premium. For most buyers, it won't — the TCL QM8 delivers comparable picture quality at a meaningfully lower price, making it the smart buy unless you specifically need four HDMI 2.1 ports.

## The Premium Segment: Samsung's Brightness Advantage

Samsung owns the premium QLED space with its QN85D and QN90D lineups, and the pricing reflects it. The **Samsung QN90D** typically runs $1,997 off-season — versus the **Hisense 65-inch U8N** at roughly $1,297. But Samsung's premium isn't arbitrary: the U8N hits 3,000 nits peak brightness, putting it in genuine HDR territory, while Samsung's QLED lineup uses advanced neural processors and wider viewing angles that justify the markup for videophiles.

Both brands use Quantum Dot film for color reproduction, but Samsung's Tizen platform offers a more polished smart TV experience than Hisense's Google TV — fewer bugs, smoother navigation, and better app optimization. The QN85D and QN90D each feature four HDMI 2.1 ports, matching the connectivity needs of serious gamers and home theater enthusiasts.

Hisense counters with aggressive pricing: the U8N delivers brightness specs that rival Samsung at $700 less. For buyers who prioritize HDR performance over smart platform polish, Hisense wins on value.
