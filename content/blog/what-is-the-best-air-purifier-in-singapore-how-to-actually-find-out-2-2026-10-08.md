## Why "best" in Singapore is a moving target

Air purifier buying in Singapore is dominated by two very different problems. The first is chronic indoor air quality: humidity, cooking odors, dust mites, and the fine particulates that come with dense urban living. The second is episodic — transboundary haze, which can turn a barely-used appliance into the most important device in the flat within days. A purifier that's "best" for quiet bedroom dust control may be badly undersized when PM2.5 spikes during haze season, and that mismatch is the single most common purchasing mistake.

The practical implication: start with room size, not brand loyalty. Manufacturers publish a recommended room coverage figure, but it's usually based on one or two air changes per hour. During a serious haze episode you want four to five air changes per hour, which effectively means dividing the advertised coverage by two for your haze-season sizing. A unit marketed for a 60 sqm living room is, realistically, a 30 sqm haze-grade purifier. If your priority is occasional smoke events rather than everyday air, size accordingly — oversizing costs more upfront but buys headroom exactly when you need it.

## The spec that matters more than the brand: CADR

Clean Air Delivery Rate is the only standardized measure of how much filtered air a purifier actually moves, and it's the number that should drive your decision. Filters capture particles; the fan determines how much air passes through them. A premium purifier with a weak fan in a large room delivers worse outcomes than a modest unit rated for that space.

Three things to check against the published CADR:

- **Match CADR to floor area.** A rough rule is that a purifier can meaningfully clean a room of roughly its smoke CADR divided by five (in square meters, assuming standard ceiling heights). Treat manufacturer "coverage" claims with suspicion if they don't publish the underlying CADR at all.
- **Check filter replacement cost per year, not per filter.** A cheap purifier with proprietary filters replaced three times a year can cost more over three years than a pricier model with affordable standard filters. This is the classic razor-and-blades trap in the category.
- **Noise at the speed you'll actually run.** The quiet-mode decibel figure is marketing; during haze you'll run the unit at max for days. Compare maximum-speed noise if you plan to use it in a bedroom or home office.

HEPA-type filtration is table stakes across reputable brands at this point — the differentiators are airflow, filter economics, and sensor accuracy, not whether the filter media captures particles in a lab test.

1. **Measure your room** and decide your primary use case: daily air quality or haze defense. If haze, halve the effective coverage.

## Red flags to avoid when shopping listings

A few patterns recur in Singapore purifier listings and are worth screening for:

- **No published CADR.** Reputable manufacturers publish it. Its absence usually means the number is unflattering.
- **"HEPA-type" or "HEPA-like" filter language.** True HEPA is a defined standard; the qualified versions are not.
- **Dramatically below-market pricing from unknown sellers.** Sometimes it's a genuine parallel-import deal; sometimes it's counterfeit or gray-stock with no local warranty. Cross-check the seller against the brand's authorized-retailer list before the price tempts you.
- **Filter subscriptions locked to the platform.** Fine if you'll stay on that platform; annoying if the brand discontinues it.
